Natural Language Processing (NLP) is a branch of artificial intelligence that focuses on the interaction between computers and human language. It enables machines to read, understand, and generate text in a meaningful way. NLP has become a foundational technology in modern AI systems, powering applications such as search engines, virtual assistants, translation services, and content recommendation platforms.

The core challenge in NLP lies in the ambiguity and complexity of natural language. Words can have multiple meanings depending on context, sentences can be structured in countless ways, and the same idea can be expressed using entirely different vocabulary. Researchers have developed a range of techniques to address these challenges, from simple statistical methods to sophisticated neural networks.

Among the most widely used classical NLP techniques is Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency, commonly known as TF-IDF. This method assigns numerical weights to words based on how frequently they appear in a specific document relative to their frequency across a larger collection. Words that appear often in a document but rarely in others receive high scores, making them good indicators of what that document is about. TF-IDF is particularly useful for information retrieval tasks such as document similarity and search ranking.

Cosine similarity is another fundamental technique used to compare documents. By representing each document as a vector in a high-dimensional space, cosine similarity measures the angle between two vectors rather than their absolute distance. This property makes it length-independent, meaning documents of different sizes can be compared fairly. When combined with TF-IDF, cosine similarity provides a robust and interpretable measure of how much two documents share in terms of their important vocabulary.

Plagiarism detection is one of the most important practical applications of document similarity. Academic institutions, publishers, and content platforms rely on automated systems to identify cases where text has been copied or closely paraphrased. A reliable plagiarism detection system must go beyond simple word matching to capture structural and semantic similarities between documents.
