Natural Language Processing, often referred to as NLP, is a field within artificial intelligence concerned with how computers process and understand human language. It allows machines to interpret and produce text in ways that are meaningful to people. NLP forms the backbone of many modern AI-driven applications, including search engines, voice assistants, language translation tools, and recommendation systems.

The fundamental difficulty in NLP is dealing with the inherent ambiguity and richness of natural language. The same word can carry different meanings in different contexts, and a single idea can be expressed in many distinct ways using different words and sentence structures. Over the years, researchers have created a wide variety of techniques to handle these issues, ranging from classical statistical models to complex deep learning architectures.

One of the most commonly applied classical techniques in NLP is Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency, or TF-IDF. This approach gives numerical importance scores to words by considering how often they appear in a given document compared to how often they appear across a broader set of documents. Words that are frequent in one document but uncommon in others are assigned higher weights, making them representative of that document's topic. TF-IDF is widely used in tasks related to document retrieval and similarity measurement.

Cosine similarity is a standard technique for measuring how alike two documents are. Each document is treated as a vector in a multi-dimensional space, and the cosine of the angle between two such vectors is used to quantify their resemblance. Because it measures direction rather than magnitude, this approach is not sensitive to document length. When used together with TF-IDF weightings, cosine similarity offers a clear and effective way to determine how much two documents overlap in their significant content.

Detecting plagiarism is one of the key real-world uses of document similarity analysis. Universities, academic journals, and online platforms use automated tools to identify text that has been duplicated or heavily paraphrased from another source. An effective plagiarism checker must look beyond exact word matches to identify cases where the structure or meaning of content has been replicated.
